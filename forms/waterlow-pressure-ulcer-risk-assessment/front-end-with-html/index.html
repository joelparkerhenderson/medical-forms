Complete the weighted categories below for an adult (16 years or over) patient at possible risk of pressure damage. Each core category (build / weight for height, skin type, sex and age, continence, mobility) and each special-risk group (tissue malnutrition, neurological deficit, major surgery or trauma, medication) contributes points; the total is the Waterlow score. A higher total means higher risk — the score maps to a risk band (low under 10, at risk 10-14, high 15-19, very high 20+) and a prevention recommendation. Answers are saved automatically in your browser as you go.

The Waterlow score is a screening prompt, not a diagnosis or staging of an existing pressure ulcer, and a low score does not remove the need for regular skin checks. Re-assess whenever the patient's condition, mobility, continence, or nutrition changes, and after any procedure that adds special-risk points.

The Waterlow score (Waterlow, Nursing Times 1985; revised 2005) is the most widely used pressure-ulcer risk tool in United Kingdom nursing and underpins NICE CG179 / QS89 and EPUAP/NPIAP guidance on pressure-ulcer prevention. This tool is a clinical decision-support aid for information only and is not a diagnosis; prevent and treat in line with local policy and clinical judgement.

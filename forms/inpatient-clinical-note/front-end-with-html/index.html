Record one clinical note against an admission episode. Pick the note type first — admission clerking, progress, consult, event, bedside procedure, handover, transfer, or discharge planning — then work through the twelve steps: note header, patient and admission, interval history, observations and NEWS2, examination, investigations reviewed, problem list, medications, risk assessments, impression, plan and escalation, and communication and sign-off. Answers are saved automatically in your browser as you go.

Two engines run over the entry. The completeness engine grades it Complete, Partial, or Incomplete against the components required for the note type you chose, and reports a percentage; an explicit negative — "no interval events", "no medication changes" — counts as documented. The acuity engine assigns a Stable, Watch, Escalate, or Critical band from NEWS2 and the deterioration markers, taking the worst finding. Safety flags fire independently of both.

A Complete grade means the record is well documented so another clinician can safely continue care; it does not mean the clinical care was correct. Neither grade is a diagnosis, and neither overrides clinical judgement.

The inpatient clinical note is a contemporaneous record against an admission episode, supporting continuity of care and good medical record-keeping (GMC Good Medical Practice; AoMRC record standards; RCP NEWS2 2017; NICE NG89 VTE, NG51 sepsis, CG103 delirium, NG15 antimicrobial stewardship). This tool grades the completeness of the written record and transcribes the published NEWS2 escalation thresholds into an acuity band; it is not a diagnosis, not a mortality or deterioration prediction, and not a substitute for clinical judgement.

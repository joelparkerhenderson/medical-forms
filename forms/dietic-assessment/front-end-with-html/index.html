Dietitian consultation record covering the five assessment domains: a medical history review, a medication and supplement check, a dietary recall of typical intake, meal frequency, portion sizes and fluid consumption, an exploration of lifestyle and food environment (work schedule, cooking skills, food budget, physical activity), and physical measurements. The engine computes a MUST score with its risk category, a GLIM malnutrition diagnosis, NRS-2002, SARC-F, SCOFF, and refeeding-syndrome risk, then a composite nutrition risk and a set of safety flags.

An initial appointment typically runs 45 to 60 minutes, and up to 90 minutes for a specialist programme. The draft is saved to this browser as you work, so a partly-filled assessment survives a page reload.

Being weighed causes distress for some patients. Step 5 accepts a declined weight as an ordinary answer and estimates the MUST body mass index component from mid-upper-arm circumference instead; the report then states that the score is an estimate. A declined weight never blocks completion.

This is clinical decision support. It does not make a diagnosis and does not replace the clinical judgement of a registered dietitian, who must sign the assessment before the report is final.

Scoring follows the BAPEN Malnutrition Universal Screening Tool ('MUST'), the GLIM consensus criteria for the diagnosis of malnutrition, NRS-2002, SARC-F, SCOFF, and the NICE CG32 refeeding-syndrome risk criteria, within the British Dietetic Association Model and Process for Nutrition and Dietetic Practice. MUST is reproduced with attribution to BAPEN. This tool is for clinical decision support; the nutrition diagnosis and care plan remain the responsibility of the registered dietitian who signs the assessment.

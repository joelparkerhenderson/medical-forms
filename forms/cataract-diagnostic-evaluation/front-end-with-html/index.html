Clinician-driven cataract diagnostic evaluation covering presenting symptoms, ocular and medical history, visual acuity, refraction, slit-lamp LOCS III grading, glare testing, tonometry, a dilated fundus examination, a competing-pathology screen (glaucoma, age-related macular degeneration, diabetic retinopathy), biometry for surgical planning, and functional / quality-of-life impact. The engine computes a LOCS III severity band per eye and a composite surgical-candidacy recommendation, then a set of safety flags.

The full evaluation typically takes 1 to 2 hours, because it requires pupil dilation and precise structural measurements of the eye. The draft is saved to this browser as you work, so a partly-filled evaluation survives a page reload.

This is clinical decision support. It does not make a diagnosis and does not replace the clinical judgement of an optometrist or ophthalmologist, who must sign the evaluation before the report is final.

Scoring follows LOCS III (Chylack LT Jr, Wolfe JK, Singer DM, et al. The Lens Opacities Classification System III. Arch Ophthalmol. 1993;111(6):831–6), a continuous four-subscale grading scale. The severity band used to drive the surgical-candidacy recommendation is this form's own operational simplification; see the NICE NG77 cataracts-in-adults guideline and the Royal College of Ophthalmologists cataract surgery guidelines. This tool is for clinical decision support; the surgical-candidacy recommendation and the referral decision remain the responsibility of the optometrist or ophthalmologist who signs the evaluation.

Apgar Score

Newborn assessment: five signs scored 0-2, summed to a total of 0-10 at 1, 5, and 10 minutes after birth.

Record the newborn's condition using the five APGAR signs — Appearance (skin colour), Pulse (heart rate), Grimace (reflex irritability), Activity (muscle tone), and Respiration — each scored 0, 1, or 2, for a total of 0 to 10 at each timepoint. Score at 1 minute and 5 minutes; if the 5-minute total is below 7, add a 10-minute timepoint (and further 5-minute scores) until the newborn stabilises. Answers are saved automatically in your browser as you go.

The Apgar score describes condition and trend, not long-term outcome. It must never delay resuscitation: when a newborn is depressed, follow the newborn-life-support algorithm and do not wait for the 1-minute score.

The Apgar score was devised by Dr Virginia Apgar (1953) and is used with the Resuscitation Council UK Newborn Life Support guidelines and NICE NG235 (Intrapartum care). This tool is for documentation and clinical support only; it is not a diagnosis and does not predict long-term outcome. Escalate and treat in line with local policy and clinical judgement.

Hip Replacement Surgery Evaluation

The orthopaedic assessment of whether a patient's hip disease and functional decline justify total hip arthroplasty, scored with the Oxford Hip Score.

Orthopaedic joint-replacement clinic evaluation covering fifteen steps: clinician and patient identification, presenting history, the validated 12-item Oxford Hip Score, functional limitations, physical examination (gait, range of motion, stability), diagnostic imaging, a conservative-treatment audit, a general-fitness screen, pre-operative baseline tests, shared decision-making, the management plan, and sign-off. The engine computes the Oxford Hip Score total and category, the surgical-candidacy recommendation, and a set of safety flags.

The draft is saved to this browser as you work, so a partly-filled evaluation survives a page reload.

This is not an ASA-grading pre-operative assessment. Step 11 records only a brief general-fitness screening note. Detailed anaesthetic-fitness assessment belongs to the sibling pre-operative assessment forms.

This is clinical decision support. It does not make a diagnosis and does not replace the clinical judgement of the orthopaedic surgeon or extended-scope physiotherapist, who must sign the evaluation before the report is final.

Scoring follows the Oxford Hip Score (Dawson J, Fitzpatrick R, Carr A, Murray D. Questionnaire on the perceptions of patients about total hip replacement. J Bone Joint Surg Br. 1996;78(2):185–190) and the Kellgren and Lawrence radiographic osteoarthritis grade (Kellgren JH, Lawrence JS. Ann Rheum Dis. 1957;16(4):494–502). This tool is for clinical decision support; the surgical-candidacy decision remains the responsibility of the orthopaedic surgeon or extended-scope physiotherapist who signs the evaluation.

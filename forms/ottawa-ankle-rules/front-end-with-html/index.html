Ottawa Ankle Rules (and Ottawa Foot Rules)

Bedside imaging decision rule: an ankle X-ray needs malleolar-zone pain AND (malleolus tenderness OR inability to bear weight); a foot X-ray needs midfoot-zone pain AND (fifth-metatarsal-base or navicular tenderness OR inability to bear weight).

Complete the Ottawa Ankle / Foot Rules criteria below for an adult (18 years or over) with an acute ankle or midfoot injury. This is a decision rule, not a score: there is nothing to add up. An ankle X-ray is indicated when there is malleolar-zone pain together with tenderness at the lateral or medial malleolus, or an inability to bear weight. A foot X-ray is indicated when there is midfoot-zone pain together with tenderness at the base of the fifth metatarsal or the navicular, or an inability to bear weight. The two decisions are independent. Answers are saved automatically in your browser as you go.

The rule is deliberately highly sensitive: a negative result reduces but does not formally exclude fracture. Apply caution in children (paediatric guidance takes precedence) and when assessment is unreliable (intoxication, distracting injury, sensory deficit, or gross swelling). Use clinical judgement alongside the computed decision.

The Ottawa Ankle Rules were derived and validated by Stiell et al. (Ann Emerg Med 1992; JAMA 1993/1994) and are one of the most widely adopted decision rules in emergency medicine. This tool is for information only and is not a diagnosis; image, safety-net, and escalate in line with local policy and clinical judgement.
